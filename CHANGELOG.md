# Changelog

All notable changes to Blockiverse VR will be documented here.

The format is based on Keep a Changelog, and releases use tags cut from `main`.

## Unreleased

- Fixed the walk head-bob appearing for a moment and then going smooth at random: it gated on `CharacterController.isGrounded`, which only reports true when the last `Move` ended in a downward collision — and XRI's gravity provider blocks gravity entirely while grounded, so flat-ground walking queued horizontal-only motion and read as airborne on most frames. The bob only ran on the frames where terrain happened to nudge the capsule down. Grounding for locomotion feedback now reads the gravity provider's per-frame sphere cast (`BlockiverseGroundedProbe`), with the controller flag kept as the fallback for rigs without one.
- Locked footsteps and the walk bob to one shared gait: a new `BlockiverseGaitCycle` on the rig owns the walk cycle, advancing its phase on horizontal travel (one step per 0.79 m) rather than on time, so cadence and bob stay in lockstep at any speed and both stop when the player walks into a wall. The bob draws the phase as `-cos`, footstep cues fire a tenth of a step ahead of the bob's low point — the head bottoms out just after heel strike, so the sound leading the view drop is what reads as a real step — and cues are rate-capped at 0.18 s so max-slider sprint (8.8 m/s, an 11 Hz crossing rate) thins rather than machine-guns. The cycle holds its phase when the player merely stops (a tapped stick accumulates toward the next footfall instead of re-arming one per tap) and never snaps it, so no reset can jump the camera; creative flight and menu-focused states suppress it entirely, which also keeps ground-skimming flight silent. Footstep clips are regenerated as noise-dominated crunches so they no longer read as the block-place cue (placeholder pending sourced audio assets).
- Fixed the creative-tools panel buttons (Fill, Replace, Delete, Copy, Paste, Undo, Redo, Set A/B, Cycle Weather) each firing their action 91 times per click: the bootstrapper wired them with a bare `AddPersistentListener`, so every regeneration stacked another copy of every handler onto the committed prefab. They now wire through the clear-then-add helper the rest of the menus already used, and a guard test pins each handler to exactly one registration. Also removed a stale pre-rename weather button that left the panel shipping two overlapping click targets.
- Made the project bootstrapper idempotent: rerunning it on a clean tree now produces a byte-identical prefab, Boot scene, and project settings (verified two consecutive runs). The Boot scene rig prefab instance is reused instead of destroyed and re-instantiated (which renumbered its fileID and dirtied the scene by ~230 lines per run), and the project's own scripting defines are written to both build targets while package-managed defines (`SENTIS_ANALYTICS_ENABLED`, `APP_UI_EDITOR_ONLY`) stay wherever their owning package puts them — naming them would oscillate against the packages' own active-target writes.

- Fixed fall-impact damage silently not charging on a frame-phase lottery: XRI's gravity grounds via its own sphere-cast, which can stop a fall with the collision capsule still hovering up to ~6 cm above the floor — `CharacterController.Move` then never reports contact, `isGrounded` stays false forever, and the landing was invisible to the vitals. Landing detection now also probes 0.1 m below the capsule base on the solid-terrain mask (fluid stays invisible, so water can never read as a landing surface). Found by the water PlayMode suite's anti-vacuity control dive; before the fluid physics layer the player could never fall at all, so the path had no coverage.
- Fixed players walking on water: fluid chunk geometry moved to a dedicated `BlockiverseFluid` physics layer (13) that gravity's ground detection cannot see — the old contact-exclusion approach (`excludeLayers`) never applied to XRI's grounded sphere-cast because Unity scene queries ignore contact-pair filtering, so every lake surface read as solid floor. The layer's physics collision-matrix row is cleared (players and props pass through), the camera renders it, the interaction ray still targets it (drink, bucket fill, block targeting all unchanged), and the teleport ray now treats the water surface as a landing spot — teleporting at a lake previously failed outright because the ray died on an unregistered collider; it now lands the player at the surface. Falling into freshwater or brine cancels fall-impact damage (water breaks the fall); emberflow does not, on top of its existing contact burn. Layer, collision matrix, prefab masks, and scenes are generated by the bootstrapper; pinned by EditMode settings/prefab tests and a PlayMode regression suite in which the player actually falls through a water surface, water never grounds the gravity provider, and a dive into water versus emberflow charges no versus full impact damage.
- Made temperature a property of place instead of sky (`voxel_world_environment_effects.md` §6.1–§6.3): the base temperature now comes from the column's biome (Dunes 34 °C down to Tundra -8 °C) rather than the current weather state, weather contributes through the typed rain (`-2 × intensity`) and snow (`-4 × intensity`) modifiers, and the altitude lapse rate rose from 0.05 to 0.15 °C per block — across the 0–48 block playable altitude band that is -7.2 °C at the tallest peak, enough for highland high ground to cross the cold-exposure threshold in clear daylight and freeze at night, where the old rate moved the whole world by only 2.4 °C. Cold exposure and weather audio/VFX query the player's own head cell; the global sky-lighting query stays positionless and uses the temperate default.
- Added per-location rain-vs-snow and wired it through the weather presentation: one storm falls as rain in a valley and as snow on the peak above it, and rain over a freezing place arrives as snow — the rain loops and splash particles switch to the snow loop and snowflake drift wherever the locally resolved kind is snow (cue selection is a pure function of the environment, pinned by EditMode tests, and re-selected from the fresh environment so a kind flip is caught even while the weather state is unchanged), while thunder stays keyed to the thunderstorm state, so a converted storm is thundersnow. The kind is derived on read from the synced weather state plus local temperature — the weather Markov chain, its transition weights, minimum durations, RNG stream, `WeatherSyncState` payload, and the v4 save schema are all unchanged, so it costs zero extra network traffic and two peers standing together always agree. The rain/snow decision deliberately runs against the temperature computed before the precipitation modifier, so a location near freezing cannot oscillate between the two answers; the published temperature can therefore sit at or below freezing while the kind stays rain, by design. Pinned by a regression test asserting the weather transition sequence is byte-identical to its pre-rework behaviour.
- Made snowpack follow what actually falls and sticks on each column instead of the sky-wide weather state and a tundra-only gate (`voxel_world_environment_effects.md` §6.3/§12): host-side sampling resolves the precipitation kind at the sampled column from that column's own biome and surface altitude, and a layer settles only where the column's temperature is at or below freezing — a rain storm over freezing high ground leaves real snow cover, a blizzard over the 34 °C dunes drifts snow VFX but lays no snowpack (falling is not settling), warm columns under rain stay bare, clear skies still cost nothing (accumulation sampling is gated on anything falling at all), and melting is unchanged.
- Gated cold-exposure damage to night or falling weather while sky-exposed (`voxel_world_environment_effects.md` §12.5, §15.4): a freezing biome in calm daylight no longer drains health the moment the world loads — tundra is a selectable starting biome, so a clear-daylight spawn stays survivable long enough to build shelter — while any snowfall on the tundra and every tundra night still bite, and heavy precipitation/storm intensity still stack extra damage sources. Night cold uses a strictly-below 3 °C threshold, chosen against the altitude lapse so the temperate starter biomes are safe on clear nights across their real terrain rather than only at sea level: a pinewild clear night is 10 - 5 - 0.15 °C per block, so an earlier 5 °C draft threshold spared exactly the shoreline while chipping health every clear night on effectively the whole forest. At 3 °C the pinewild floor stays safe through +13 blocks, clear-night pressure starts at +14 (2.9 °C), meadow and wetland cannot reach it inside the 0–48 playable relief band, and highlands and tundra nights stay dangerous — pinned at representative pinewild altitudes, and the threshold's own boundary is pinned to the safe side.
- Unified day/night on one canonical window (`WorldConstants` DayStart 0.05 / NightStart 0.55 with `IsDay`/`IsNight`): `WorldTimeClock` aliases it and `WeatherService` delegates to it, removing a second night predicate (`t > 0.6 || t < 0.1`) that disagreed with the clock for 10% of every day and would have offset the -5 °C night modifier and the night-cold damage window from visible dusk.
- Fixed the controller pointer ray pointing ~30° below where the controller aims. The controller transform rides the OpenXR **grip** pose and the ray origin was a controller child pitched a fixed 90° down from it, but Meta's own pointer rides the **aim** pose, which on Touch controllers sits ~60° below the grip forward with a ~5° inward yaw and ~5 cm of positional offset (measured live through Meta XR Operator `openxr_get_controller_pose` grip vs aim, `touch_controller_plus` profile: 30.4° of error on both hands). The ray origin is now kept on the aim pose by `BlockiverseAimPoseRayOrigin`, which reads the grip and aim poses from the rig's existing `Position`/`Rotation`/`Aim Position`/`Aim Rotation` actions each frame and applies their rigid offset in controller-local space — jitter-free regardless of update order, held through tracking blips, and falling back to the measured per-hand offset until the first tracked sample. The earlier attempt at this (sibling aim transforms under Camera Offset) broke controller tracking because the runtime repair kept destroying them and their drivers disabled the shared `Tracking State` action on the way out; this design creates no objects the repair scrubs and never disables shared actions. Verified in the Meta XR Simulator: the Unity ray origin's local pose matches the Operator-derived grip→aim offset to four decimals on both hands.
- Enabled the Meta Quest Touch Plus and Touch Pro OpenXR interaction profiles for the Standalone (editor) target in the bootstrapper, matching Android. The Meta XR Simulator presents Touch Plus/Pro controllers and the runtime binds those profiles, so with only the legacy Oculus Touch profile enabled Unity's controller actions stayed unbound in Play mode — the runtime tracked the controllers (visible through Meta XR Operator) while the rig never moved, which made simulator sessions useless for any input or ray validation.

- Fixed sprint and crouch doing nothing in the title mini-world. Both gated on `AllowWorldInput`, which is false whenever a menu holds input focus — the entire title state — so the player could walk around the mini-world but could neither sprint nor crouch. They are locomotion modifiers, not world-editing actions, so they now follow the same availability rule as movement itself.
- Added independent control-style settings for sprint and crouch. Both default to **click-and-hold** (active only while the stick click is held); the new **Click To Toggle Sprint** and **Click To Toggle Crouch** comfort checkboxes switch each one separately to click-to-toggle, so holding to sprint while crouch is a toggle is a valid setup. This replaces the previous behavior where a single click both toggled *and* a hold temporarily engaged, which made a long press ambiguous and could leave the modifier latched on. Turning a toggle checkbox off clears any latched state, so a stale toggle can never sprint or crouch the player in hold mode.
- Fixed the Quest system keyboard never opening on Quest. The Android entry point was `GameActivity`, which breaks Unity's soft-keyboard handshake: the request reached the Quest IME (`onShowInputRequested`) but the window only ever opened `isCandidatesOnly=true` and Unity logged `Timeout while waiting for keyboard to become visible`. The generated manifest's activity class **and** theme now follow `PlayerSettings.Android.applicationEntry` — classic `Activity` uses `@style/UnityThemeSelector`, since the AppCompat themes only link when GameActivity pulls in androidx.appcompat and leaving one behind fails Android resource linking outright. Device-verified: the IME now opens `isCandidatesOnly=false` and accepts typing. Note Meta's Project Setup Tool flags GameActivity as "Required"; that recommendation re-breaks the keyboard and must not be applied.

- Fixed night being almost pitch black and placed light sources emitting nothing. Six independent layers were suppressing light, and all of them had to be undone: the generated Quest URP asset disabled additional lights (`m_AdditionalLightsRenderingMode: 0`, per-object limit `0`) and all shadows (`m_ShadowDistance: 0`, both shadow-supported flags `0`); `BlockiverseProjectBootstrapper.ConfigureQuestUrpShadowPolicy` wrote those values, so any hand-fix would revert on the next bootstrap; the `Blockiverse/Voxel Lit` shader never looped additional lights, called the no-shadow `GetMainLight()` overload, and had no `ShadowCaster` pass; `VoxelWorldRenderer` set `shadowCastingMode = Off` and `receiveShadows = false` on every chunk; `BlockiverseLightingCycleController` force-wrote `shadows = None` every `LateUpdate`; and the single directional light pitched a full 360°, so at midnight it pointed straight up from *under* the terrain and N·L was zero on every ground face regardless of its intensity.
- Added a moon. The sun and moon now share the scene's one directional light — URP only ever promotes a single directional to the main light, so a second one would silently become a costly additional light — and whichever body is above the horizon drives it. Moon phase follows the canonical 8-day cycle from `docs/rulesets/voxel_world_environment_effects.md` §4.4, derived from `WorldTimeClock.TotalElapsedTicks / 24000`, which already travels in the host's environment snapshot and in the save file: the lunar cycle is deterministic and lockstep across host/client and save/load with **no** wire-format or save-schema change. Night radiance is now tied to the ruleset's 0–15 sky-light scale — full moon 4/15 (~27% of daylight), quarter/gibbous 3/15, crescent 2/15, new moon 1/15 — instead of the previous ~2%.
- Fixed placed glowwicks, lumen lamps, campfires, and spark flares silently getting no light object at all in survival worlds. `GlowwickLightManager` filled its 24-light budget by iterating an unordered `HashSet` populated in ascending-Y scan order, so world-generated emberflow below y=18 claimed every slot before the scan ever reached the surface, and slots were never revisited when the player moved. Slots are now spent on the emitters nearest the viewer, a freshly placed emitter can evict a more distant one, and reselection is throttled — deliberately O(budget) rather than O(all emitters), because emberflow is a live fluid that fires `BlockChanged` every simulation tick.
- Emitter light range and brightness now follow the canonical emissive levels: range in world units equals the block's `EmissiveLight` (§5.3 propagates one level per block, and one block is one Unity unit), so a glowwick reaches 9 blocks instead of the previous 4, and intensity is proportional to level so the ladder emberflow < campfire < lumen lamp < spark flare reads as brightness instead of being compressed by an additive floor.
- Voxel terrain, players, and placed emitters now cast and receive shadows, budgeted for Quest rather than enabled wholesale: main-light shadows at 1024 with a single cascade over 30 m, hard shadows only (Unity flags soft shadows as significant on tile-based mobile/XR GPUs and Meta's guidance is hard-or-none), additional lights per-pixel capped at 4 per object, and only the nearest few emitters casting shadows — point lights render six cube faces each, so an unbounded budget would have cost several hundred extra draws. The translucent block-placement ghost is excluded, or it would have thrown a solid block shadow that followed the player's aim.
- Fixed a latent stereo bug in the voxel shader that the lighting work would have activated: it declared none of the instancing/stereo macros, so the fragment stage's `unity_StereoEyeIndex` stayed 0. Harmless while the fragment read nothing eye-dependent, but the light-cluster lookup is indexed by eye, so the right eye would have sampled the left eye's lights. Also corrected the URP asset's shader-variant prefiltering, which was set to **strip** the shadow and additional-light keywords at build time — the classic "works in the editor, black on device" failure.
- Lit the natural cave crystals. `lumen_quartz_cluster` and `staropal_geode` are listed as light sources in the rulesets (§5.3: 7 and 5 of 15) but had shipped registered at `0`, so caves stayed dark even though the light manager already carried bespoke cyan and violet colours for both. They now emit their canonical levels. This is a deliberate gameplay change as well as a lighting one: crop growth reads the same sampled light level, so **berries now grow directly adjacent to lumen quartz, and reeds adjacent to either crystal** — grain stays out of reach (its minimum is 8), and the effect drops off after one block. Existing saves are unaffected; the save registry hash covers canonical IDs only, not block attributes.
- Light no longer passes through solid blocks. Realtime point lights in URP are pure inverse-square with no occlusion — only a shadow map stops them, and emitter shadows are rationed to the nearest one for Quest — so every other glowwick lit the far side of walls and the caves beneath it. The mesh bake now traces a voxel line-of-sight ray from each face to every emitter in range and stores the result in the vertex colour's green channel; the shader multiplies the realtime term by it, so a torch outside a wall, or on the surface above a cave, contributes nothing behind the solid. At block resolution this is indistinguishable from every emitter casting shadows, for zero GPU cost. Sky light is now a separate channel (red) that emitters can no longer raise, so a torch in a cave no longer lets sunlight in, and its floor has dropped from 20% to **0**: a sealed room with no window or light source is dark, and a tunnel fades to nothing by twelve blocks in. Emitter blocks carry their own level in the blue channel so a torch stays visible in the dark it now creates. Chunk rebuild invalidation widened from 13 to 17 blocks to cover the brightest emitter's reach.

- Fixed the player's size and crouch on Quest: the collision capsule is now owned by the game (`BlockiversePlayerBodyManipulator`) instead of being resized to the tracked camera height on every move by XRI's stock manipulator, which made the player's size depend on who was wearing the headset and silently undid crouch. Players are two blocks tall standing and one block crouched by default; crouch (dominant stick click, tap to toggle or hold) now physically shrinks the capsule and eases the view down. Added an opt-in **Use My Real Height** comfort setting where collision and view follow the player's tracked height, so tall players duck or crouch where shorter players walk through.
